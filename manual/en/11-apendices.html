AThe files the app reads and writes

PollinationPro runs in the browser and does not send your data to any server: every file comes in from and goes out to a folder on your computer. Only name resolution, the GBIF download and the search for published interactions query the internet, and only when you ask for them.

A.1 · What the app reads

FormatWhere and what for
.csv, .txt, .tsvBlock 2, occurrence records and visit observations. Any separator, UTF-8 or Windows-1252; the columns are recognized by their heading in Spanish or English, or by the Darwin Core names.
.xlsxBlock 2, the same, from a spreadsheet; if the workbook has several sheets, you choose which one. The reader is written by the program itself from the public specification of the format.
pastedBlock 2, …or paste the table: the cells copied from a spreadsheet with their row of headings.
project .jsonBlock 2, 📂 Open a project: the list, the records, the visits, the interactions and the filters saved earlier by the app.
.tif, .tiff (GeoTIFF)Blocks 7 and 8, current and future environmental layers: uncompressed, LZW, Deflate or PackBits, with predictors; in strips or tiles; classic or BigTIFF; in degrees.
.asc (ASCII grid)Blocks 7 and 8, the same in the text format that any geographic information system writes.
style .jsonBlock 4, Load a style…: a map style saved earlier.

CGlossary

TermMeaning
AUCArea under the ROC curve: the probability that a cell with a record receives higher suitability than a random background cell.
Background (fondo)The cells of available environment against which what the species use is compared.
Centroid (centroide)The point assigned when only the country or the state is known: the area centroid or the center of the bounding box.
Chao1Estimator of the minimum number of species (here, of visitors) from those seen once and twice.
Circular statistics (estadística circular)Statistics that treat dates as angles, because the year is a circle.
Clip (recorte)Drawing the map only inside the outline of Mexico or of the chosen states.
Co-occurrence (coocurrencia)Two taxa having records at the same site (cell). It is an opportunity for interaction, not an interaction.
Completeness (completitud)Recorded visitors divided by estimated visitors: how much of the sampling has already been done.
Connectance (conectancia)Observed links divided by possible links of a network.
Convex hull (envolvente convexa)The smallest polygon without indentations that encloses all the points of a unit.
Cross-validation (validación cruzada)Evaluating a model with records that were not used to fit it.
d′Specialization of a species with respect to the availability of its partners, from 0 to 1.
Environmental space (espacio ambiental)The plane of the first two principal components of the climate variables of the background.
GBIFGlobal Biodiversity Information Facility: the portal that gathers occurrence records from collections and observations around the world.
H₂′Specialization of a whole quantitative network, from 0 to 1.
List entry (entrada de la lista)Each name of the study list (species, genus or family), with its role.
Living collection (colección viva)A specimen from a botanical garden or a field genebank; it does not say where the wild population lives.
Mismatch (desajuste)Area suitable for the plant and for no potential visitor.
Modularity Q (modularidad Q)How much a network divides into groups that interact more among themselves.
NODFMeasure of the nestedness of a matrix, from 0 to 100.
Null model (modelo nulo)The random version of the data that keeps what is not in question (the totals, the geography of sampling) and breaks only what is being tested.
Omission (omisión)Fraction of validation records that fall outside the suitable area.
Opportunity for interaction (oportunidad de interacción)Coincidence in space, time and environment: a necessary, not sufficient, condition for pollination.
Potential pollinator (polinizador potencial)A visitor that coincides with the plant in space, time or environment, but whose visits and pollen transport have not been documented.
Published interaction (interacción publicada)A plant–visitor pair recorded in the literature or in collections and gathered by a database of biotic interactions.
R̄Length of the mean vector of the dates: their concentration, from 0 to 1.
Rayleigh test (prueba de Rayleigh)Test that a set of dates is seasonal and not uniform over the year.
Role (papel)Plant or visitor.
Sampling bias (sesgo de muestreo)The uneven distribution of recording effort: near roads, cities and stations.
Schoener’s D (D de Schoener)Overlap of two probability distributions (monthly, elevational or in environmental space), from 0 to 1.
SES (EEF)Standardized effect size: (observed − expected) / null standard deviation.
Suitability (idoneidad)How much the environment of a cell resembles that of the records of a unit, according to a model.
Synonym (sinónimo)An earlier name of the same taxon; the app gathers them under the current name and keeps the original.
Target group (grupo objetivo)The sites (or months) where any taxon of the study was recorded: the universe that corrects for sampling bias.
Threshold (umbral)The suitability value from which a cell is declared suitable.
TSSSensitivity + specificity − 1 of a binary map, from −1 to 1.
Unit of analysis (unidad de análisis)What Blocks 4 to 8 compare: a list entry or a species.
Universe (universo)The set of sites among which chance is distributed in a co-occurrence test.
Veech test (prueba de Veech)Exact hypergeometric test of the number of sites shared by two taxa.
Watson–Williams test (prueba de Watson–Williams)Test that two sets of dates have the same mean date.

DWhen something does not work

What happensWhat to do
The app opens blank or without stylesA folder is missing (js, css, data). Copy the whole program folder.
Names cannot be resolved or downloadedNo internet, or GBIF does not respond. Try later; meanwhile, use the practice data or your own records.
A name was resolved as another taxon (a fossil, another kingdom)Choose the right one in the selector of that name (Section 2.2).
The download was cut off halfwayRepeat Download the records: only the GBIF records of each name are replaced.
My columns are not recognizedChoose each field in the mapping, or download the template and use its headings.
My dates come out with the day and month swappedChange Dates written as to day/month/year or month/day/year.
There are points in the sea or in another countryCheck the coordinate rules in Block 3; “In the sea or outside the outlines” and “Outside the stated country” are set to flag only by default.
Almost all pairs come out “unclassified”Too few records for the cell: use larger cells or merge species into their genus.
Block 7 does not read a layerIt must be in degrees (latitude and longitude). Reproject layers in meters.
The layers look shifted or come out “resampled”They have different grids. Use layers from the same source and resolution.
Block 8 says that future layers are missingName the future files with the same ending as the current ones (“bio_1”…).
The network has no H₂′ or d′You mixed your own visits with published interactions: use only your visits for those indices.
A figure looks different from how I left itCheck the ✎ editor of that figure and the figure studio: the changes are kept in the browser. Undo everything on this figure returns it to how the app draws it.
The report lacks a blockThat block is not computed: open it (it computes itself on opening) and go back to Block 10.
The browser blocks many downloadsExport the figures as a single .zip from the catalog of Block 10.
I want to start from scratchEmpty the list in Block 2 clears the study; Reset everything in the map styles returns them to their starting point.

FThe tool: license and how to cite it

F.1 · What the app carries inside

PollinationPro is written in HTML, CSS and JavaScript without any third-party library: not for the charts, not for the maps, not for reading GeoTIFF files or spreadsheets, not for writing the .zip. The methods are those of the original publications, cited in the code, on the home page and in the report. The outlines of the countries and of the states of Mexico come from Natural Earth (public domain). The practice data and the practice layers are fictional and are generated by the program itself. The file PROCEDENCIA.md in the program folder details the origin of everything, tests/index.html checks the engine with 168 tests, and the validation/ folder compares it number by number with R packages written by other people (34,299 comparisons, none in disagreement).
